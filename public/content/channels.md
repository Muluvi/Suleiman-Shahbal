## 12.1 Where the effort goes

Because **55.0% of the county register sits in the three constituencies decided by fewer than 2,000 votes in 2022** (Section 6.2), output and targeting are weighted accordingly. The campaign cannot rely on reach on the Island spilling over to the mainland estates.

**What the message says there.** In these wards the Builder Governor message leads on water hours, jobs with an employer's name, and paid bills. Calling any ward neglected needs the county's development record by ward, which is not yet in hand (Section 3.7); until it is, the campaign says what it will publish, not what the county failed to deliver.

**Where the voters are, and where turnout fell.** Turnout was lowest in Jomvu and Changamwe on 29 August 2022 (Tier 2, The Star), and the three closest constituencies hold 7 of the twelve largest wards (Section 6.2).

## 12.4 Which language reaches which voters

```lead
**Kiswahili is the language of trust, argument and mobilisation in Mombasa**; English carries policy and the national press; Sheng carries the young; Mijikenda languages carry the mainland's older households.
```

Mombasa speaks Kiswahili first: Kimvita, the Mombasa dialect, is the city's own. English is the language of statutory policy and the national press; Sheng is how the young talk online and at the stages; Mijikenda languages, Digo and Giriama among them, are spoken in many mainland households. Each is mapped to its audience, its main channel, its register and its own native-speaker sign-off.


**Which number governs.** The shares in the language map describe who speaks what (Tier 3, an estimate); the target for what the campaign publishes is the content mix below. Bulk SMS carries Kiswahili and English only, by law (Section 15.3.3), which in Mombasa matters little, because Kiswahili is the first language of the campaign anyway.

* **Kiswahili** — the default for voice notes, radio, video, SMS, USSD and the stage. Coastal idiom and Kimvita expressions where they fit; every line checked by a native speaker from Mombasa.
* **English** — policy papers, the national press, LinkedIn, the diaspora.
* **Sheng** — TikTok, Instagram and youth video, written by young people from the estates, reviewed for clarity.
* **Mijikenda languages** — radio spots on Radio Kaya, voice notes and in person on the mainland; never by bulk SMS.
