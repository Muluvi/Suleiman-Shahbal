## 3.7 How county money is spent

```lead
Mombasa carried **KSh 4.4 billion** in pending bills in 2024, the third-highest of any county, and **98%** of what it owes is more than three years old (Tier 2, the Controller of Budget as reported).
```

**Pending bills.** The Controller of Budget reported Mombasa's pending bills at **KSh 4.4 billion** in 2024, KSh 2.7 billion recurrent and KSh 1.7 billion development, the third-highest of the 47 counties (Tier 2, as reported). At 30 June 2026 the figure was about **KSh 3.8 billion**, almost all owed by the executive, and the Controller found that neither arm kept to its payment plan (Tier 3). **98%** of the county's outstanding bills are more than three years old, the highest proportion in Kenya (Tier 2, Nation, reporting the Controller).

**The audit record.** The Auditor-General gave the County Executive a qualified opinion for FY2024/25 and the County Assembly an unmodified one (Tier 3, as reported). The queries, as reported from the Auditor-General's FY2024/25 summary (Tier 3): expenditure without supporting documents KSh 414m, inaccuracies in the financial statements KSh 113m, an unreconciled gap between the cash book and the bank of KSh 64m, and contracts worth KSh 95m with procurement irregularities. Revenue owed to the county stood at KSh 13.68bn at 30 June 2026, 86% of it in unpaid land rates (Controller of Budget, Tier 3). Audit queries are questions the county must answer, not findings of theft.

**Where the money went in FY2025/26.** Wages took **55.1%** of revenue against a legal ceiling of 35%, and only **45.8%** of the development budget was spent: KSh 2.57bn, down from KSh 3.43bn the year before (Controller of Budget, Tier 3, as reported).

**The county's own books.** By its own count the county owed **KSh 3.75bn** in pending bills on 1 July 2025, of which KSh 1.29bn is unpaid salary arrears and statutory deductions; **82%** of the stock dates from before FY2024/25. Its plan for FY2025/26 set aside KSh 400m to pay them; the approved budget gave KSh 119m. In FY2024/25 it received KSh 14.80bn of the KSh 17.36bn it budgeted (85%) (Tier 1, ADP 2026/27).

**The plan the county cannot pay for.** The 2026/27 development plan costs its departments' projects at **KSh 13.76bn** and expects **KSh 4.76bn** to pay for them: a gap of **KSh 9.00bn**, or **65%** unfunded, before a single ward project or flagship is counted. The flagships add **KSh 55.1bn** across 20 projects; 16 of them depend on the Mombasa Investment Corporation finding an investor. The one industrial project under way, the Miritini aggregation centre, is 35% complete, KSh 106m spent of KSh 500m (Tier 1, ADP 2026/27).

**How money is split between wards.** The County Assembly passed a County Equitable Share Bill that would have shared 30% of the roughly **KSh 4 billion** annual development budget equally across the 30 wards; Speaker Aharub Khatri criticised the governor for rejecting it, and an audit found stalled projects and growing pending bills (Tier 2, The Standard). A ward-by-ward record of county spending is not yet in hand.

**What it means for the campaign.** A county that borrows to pay salaries and owes suppliers for more than three years is the opening for a candidate whose case is that he pays his bills and finishes what he starts. The commitment has to be concrete: a published pending-bills schedule, paid in order, oldest first.
