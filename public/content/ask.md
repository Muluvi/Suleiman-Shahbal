Suleiman Shahbal has the record, the persistence and the plan to be an
outstanding governor of Mombasa County. He also has something rare in county politics:
things he has built that voters can go and see. Water bought by the jerrican, pending bills
older than three years, and salaries paid on an overdraft are what the record shows for the
county (Sections 3.1 and 3.7), and his record of building is currently the campaign's most
under-used asset.

**But the timeline is not the one the planning cycle assumes.** He contests 2027 without ODM
or UDA, and just over half the register, 55.0%, lives in the three constituencies decided
by fewer than 2,000 votes in 2022. The working hypothesis is that the gap is one of turnout and
machinery rather than recognition (Section 7.1). If Week 1 confirms it, it is the kind of gap
that closes through a disciplined ground and SMS operation in the close-contest belt.

This plan sets out the platform that lets his own team turn an active presence into a directed one: an audit of
what the existing accounts are actually doing (Section 14.1), a strategy derived from it, a weekly
brief the team writes and carries to the people already publishing, an SMS and USSD layer built for the
53.1% of Mombasa residents who are not online, a published ethics charter that turns
careful data practice into a differentiator (Section 19.3), a data-protection gate that protects
the candidate from the legal exposure others in this cycle will walk into (Section 19.2), and a
public delivery tracker that puts his strongest credential to work before a single vote is
cast.

Firefly recommends the Standard scope level. His team then enters its own ninety-day export, and everything after that is evidence for the team to act on.

#### What the record does not yet show

Mr Shahbal is already in the conversation: he has been since 2013. What the record does not yet show
is a reason behind any single post: which voter it was for, which of the **353,090** registered voters
in the close-contest belt it reached, and whether it moved anything.

## 23.1 The clock that sets the pace

> **What sets the pace.** The IEBC's Gazette deadlines and election day, 10 August 2027. Those dates are not in the campaign's gift, and every other date in this plan is derived from them rather than chosen. For a candidate on a smaller party's ticket, the party's own deadlines come first: certified membership lists by 23 March 2027 and nominations from 3 June 2027.
>
> **What the work needs in front of it.** Roughly six weeks for a first directed cycle, and the first of those six for the presence audit that sets its targets.
>
> **What is lost by waiting.** Each week of delay is a week less of directed output in front of the
> window, and a week of the audit's ninety-day window filling with output nobody briefed.
