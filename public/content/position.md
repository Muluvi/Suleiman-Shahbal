**Suleiman Shahbal is the Builder Governor Mombasa needs**: not a politician who merely promises, but a builder who can show what he built and the jobs it made. Mombasa does not lack money or ambition; it lacks water at the tap, bills paid on time, and work that stays in the county, and building things that work is his profession. This chapter states the claim and the paradox it answers.

## 9.1 The central claim

```lead
Mombasa voters are ready for a governor who delivers, fixes what is broken, pays what the county owes, and is willing to be measured.
```

**Why this claim.** What distinguishes Mr Shahbal is not a vote tally but his record of building (Section 6.1): a bank, a group, and an industrial zone signed with a global port operator. So the campaign's core narrative is that **Suleiman Shahbal is the Builder Governor Mombasa needs**: someone who has built institutions that still stand and can be held to account for building the county's. It rests on one premise, in his own words: Mombasa needs managers who can deliver, fix what is broken and move the port city forward (Tier 2, as reported, September 2026).

**What makes it his.** Many candidates promise jobs. He can point to a signed agreement, a site and a projected number, 7,972 direct jobs in Jomvu (Tier 2, a projection). This plan turns that into a public delivery tracker (Section 15.1.1): the projection beside the jobs actually created, and the county's water and pending bills beside his commitments, so a line in a biography becomes a public test he invites.

**The governing line:** *"Mombasa has the port, the people and the money. What it has lacked is leadership that finishes what it starts — and shows the receipts."*

## 9.2 The resource paradox

```lead
**Mombasa has a port economy and a KSh 18.73 billion budget; its residents buy water by the jerrican and its suppliers wait years to be paid.** The obstacle is not money. It is delivery.
```

**Mombasa has the resources; what it lacks is delivery.** The county's FY2025/26 budget was **KSh 18.73 billion**, of which it raised **KSh 6.21 billion** itself (Tier 3, Section 1.5). Against it, the city receives about **20.0%** of the water it needs and loses **60%** of what it buys (Tier 2, Section 3.1); the county owed **KSh 4.4 billion** in pending bills in 2024, **98%** of them more than three years old (Tier 2), and borrowed to pay June 2026 salaries (Tier 3, Section 3.7).

The county's obstacle is not a lack of funding, but a lack of delivery: water reticulation, project completion, and paying suppliers in order. These are the competencies a builder brings, and the ones the campaign asks voters to measure him on.
